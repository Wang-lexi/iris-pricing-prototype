## Length and format

Keep it short. I will read every executive summary and every main report, and I will go into appendices only where I want more.

- **Executive summary (required): one page.** Write it last. The problem, your recommended approach, and what you need from your partner. It must make sense to someone who has seen nothing else, and it can stand alone.
- **Main report: 4 pages maximum for Draft 2, 5 pages for the Dec 6 version.** Single-spaced, 11-point font, standard margins. In Markdown, that is about 600 words per page (roughly 2,400 words for Draft 2, 3,000 for Dec 6). The executive summary is separate and does not count toward this.
- **Appendices: encouraged, no page limit.** Put your working and detail there, reference each one from the report, and write the report so it makes sense without them. I will not read all of them closely.

## Main report: the sections I want to see

Use these as headings, in this order. Keep each one tight: the conclusion and the key reasoning, not the full working.

1. **The managerial problem.** What is going wrong, for whom, and why, beyond the request you were given. What doing nothing costs and what improvement is worth. The framing you chose and, in a sentence each, the others you rejected.
2. **Top three assumptions.** The three that would change your plan most if wrong, their status (untested, partner-confirmed, or disproved), and how you will test each.
3. **Approach and prototype.** What you recommend and why it fits this problem and not another. What the prototype does, what it does not do, and what it taught you. Everyone on the team must be able to explain it.
4. **People and adoption.** Who would run this, who is affected, who must agree, and who might resist.
5. **Success measures.** How you will judge it, the bar, and what would make you change course.
6. **What changed since Draft 1.** A short log: what you changed and why, what you dropped, and what your partner or I pushed back on. This is the most important section for showing learning.
7. **Open questions and asks.** What you still need to find out, and from whom.

Data and constraints (including which AI tools your partner permits) and the Winter plan can go in the report in a few lines each if they matter to your argument; otherwise put them in an appendix.

## Appendices

Suggested set (add or drop as fits your work, but label each one and reference it from the report):

- **A. Diagnostic and framings:** 5 Whys or issue tree, and the alternative framings you rejected.
- **B. Assumption table:** each assumption with its status (untested, partner-confirmed, or disproved).
- **C. Data and constraints:** data inventory, access status, privacy and confidentiality notes, and gap handling.
- **D. Prototype:** description, how to run it, results on sample or synthetic data, and the skill file or `agent.md` (scope, triggers, success criteria). Link to the repo files too.
- **E. Winter plan:** week-by-week plan through Apr 2, partner dependencies, and risk register (data delays, tool costs, scope changes).
- **F. AI use note:** which tools you used, for what, and where the team made the call.
- **G. Evidence:** partner answers, notes and anything else that supports the body.
